01 Section 2.1

Coastal Tourism & Economy

Economic opportunities offered by coastal environments alongside significant natural vulnerabilities.

Economic Drivers:
  • Attractions: Beaches, water sports, eco-tourism.
  • Nature Reserves: Wildlife preservation & bio-tourism.
  • Real Estate: High-value seafront properties & rentals.
Key Vulnerabilities & Hazards:
  • Hurricanes, storm surges, tsunamis, sea-level rise.
  • Land loss forcing community relocation.
  • Saltwater Intrusion: Contaminates freshwater aquifers used for drinking & irrigation.
02 Section 2.2

Tropical Storms & The 4 Ps

Systematic framework for reducing storm risks: Preparation, Planning, Protection, Prediction.

Preparation: Emergency response plans, stockpiling supplies, evacuation route education, securing loose objects.
Planning: Long-term land-use zoning (avoiding high-risk areas) and building codes.
Protection: Hard engineering (sea walls, levees) and soft buffers (mangroves).
Prediction: Satellite tracking, weather modeling, early warning broadcasts.
03 Section 2.3

Coral Reef Ecosystems

'Rainforests of the sea' providing critical biodiversity, economic value, and coastal buffer.

Distribution & Conditions:

Located between 30° N and 30° S in warm, salty, clear ocean waters.

Coral Bleaching Mechanics:

Thermal stress causes corals to expel symbiotic microalgae living in their tissues. Persistently high water temperatures lead to coral mortality.

Management Examples:

Galápagos MPA: Limits tourists & uses entry fees for conservation. Maldives: Coral propagation & fragment transplanting.

04 Section 2.3

Mangrove Forests

Salt-tolerant coastal ecosystems providing shoreline stabilization and carbon storage.

Key Role & Carbon Sink:

Roots trap sediment & absorb storm energy. India's Sundarbans stores ~4,000 metric tons of carbon/ha.

Threats:

Deforestation (Vietnam/Indonesia), Hooghly River industrial pollution, sea-level rise in low islands (Kiribati).

Sustainable Aquaculture:

Integrated mangrove-shrimp farming in Indonesia balances economic yield with habitat preservation.

05 Case Study 2.4

Florida Geology & Erosion

Causes and state-level statistics regarding critical beach erosion in Florida, USA.

Physical Geography:

Flattest US state (max elevation 105 m). Composed of easily eroded limestone and sandstone with zero natural cliff barriers.

Erosion Stat (FDEP July 2023):

Out of 1,328 km of sandy beaches, 696 km is classified as critically eroded, threatening development & habitats.

Impacted Hotspots:

Miami Beach, Fort Lauderdale, Naples, Sarasota, Everglades, Florida Keys.

06 Case Study 2.4

Florida Coastal Protection

Financial expenditures, soft living shorelines, and Hurricane Idalia mitigation outcomes.

Nourishment Costs:

Since 1935, over $1.9 billion spent. State & local governments spend $60M–$100M/year ($30M–$50M each).

Living Shorelines & Engineering:

Miami Beach 2022–2023 project ($40.4M at $50/yd³ sand). Virginia Key volunteers planted native trees (May 2022).

Hurricane Idalia (Aug 2023):

Category 4 at Keaton Beach (185 km/h winds, 2.7m surge). FDEM early warnings kept fatalities to 10 despite $3.6B damage.

Hard vs. Soft Engineering Matrix

Interactive comparison of coastal defense techniques, longevity, financial costs, visual impacts, and mechanics.

Strategy Category Mechanics Longevity Cost / Maintenance Drawbacks & Impacts
Sea Walls Hard Massive concrete structures that physically reflect wave energy back to sea. Up to 50 yrs Very High Initial & Repair Emsores, expensive maintenance, high wave reflection causes toe erosion.
Groynes Hard Wooden/stone barriers at 90° to shore trapping sediment from longshore drift. Up to 40 yrs Moderate to High Starves down-drift beaches of sediment, increasing erosion downstream.
Rock Armour / Rip-rap Hard Heavy granite boulders absorbing wave energy while letting backwash drain through gaps. Long-lasting Expensive (Imported) Visually unappealing, transport costs are high, hazardous for beachgoers.
Revetments Hard Sloped wooden or concrete fences that absorb energy and break wave impact. 10-30 yrs Expensive to build, cheap maintenance Intrusive aesthetic impact, restricts beach access.
Gabions Hard Wire mesh cages filled with small stones placed at cliff bases. 5-10 yrs Low Initial Cost Metal cages rust easily; less durable against powerful storm waves.
Breakwaters Hard Submerged or offshore stone mounds built parallel to shore to force wave breaking. 30+ yrs Very High Construction Can be overtopped at high tide, disrupts navigation, alters currents.
Beach Nourishment Soft Adding sand/shingle from inland or seabed to widen natural beach buffers. Short (1-5 yrs) High Recurring (FL: $30M-$50M/yr) Requires continuous replenishment; transport costs high ($50/yd³).
Managed Retreat Soft Allowing low-value land to flood naturally, establishing saltmarsh habitats. Permanent / Long Low Maintenance Requires financial compensation for displaced landowners & farmers.

Shoreline Management Plan (SMP) Policies

POLICY 1

Hold the existing defence line

Maintain current engineering structures to retain shoreline in its present position.

POLICY 2

Advance the existing line

Build new defenses seaward of the existing line to reclaim eroded land.

POLICY 3

Managed realignment

Allow coastline to move naturally inland under control, creating natural salt marsh buffers.

POLICY 4

No active intervention

Do not invest in defenses or maintenance; allow natural coastal processes to take over.

Florida Coastal Management & Hurricane Idalia

2nd Longest US Coastline | Flattest State (Max 105m)

Core Case Study
CRITICAL EROSION (FDEP 2023) 696 km out of 1,328 km beaches
ANNUAL NOURISHMENT COST $60M–$100M ($30M-$50M state + local)

Geology & Causes: Soft limestone and sandstone rocks easily erode under wave energy from Atlantic Ocean and Gulf of Mexico. Urbanization interferes with longshore drift.

Miami Beach Army Corps Project (2022-2023): $40.4 million spent trucking inland sand at $50 per cubic yard.

Hurricane Idalia (August 2023): Landfall at Keaton Beach as Cat 4 (185 km/h winds, 2.7m storm surge). FDEM early warnings & states of emergency restricted fatalities to 10 despite $3.6 billion in structural damage.

Living Shorelines: Virginia Key tree planting (May 2022) & salt marsh restoration via Florida Coastal Management Program (FCMP).

Cyclone Idai (Southern Africa)

March 2019 | Mozambique, Malawi & Zimbabwe

LIC / MIC Context
FATALITIES 1,000+ Deaths 2nd deadliest southern storm
CROP DESTRUCTION 715,000 ha affected 1.5M people

Primary Impacts: Torrential rainfall, severe inland and coastal flooding, total destruction of poorly reinforced residential housing stock.

Secondary Impacts: Widespread waterborne cholera outbreaks from contaminated standing floodwaters; catastrophic agricultural loss leading to severe food insecurity across the region.

🌴

Hurricane Irma Comparison

Preparedness Differential: Florida vs. Caribbean

Caribbean Impact: Struck Caribbean islands at Category 5 (highest possible rating). Despite hurricane-resistant home designs, wind speeds exceeded structural tolerance thresholds, causing total devastation on islands like Barbuda. Disaster aid from UK/French/Dutch governments faced severe delays.

Florida Mitigation: Downgraded to Category 4 near Florida Keys. Early prediction gave 1 week warning—allowing residents to board up windows, install shutters, and execute orderly evacuations. Strict building code compliance avoided widespread collapse.

Galápagos & Sundarbans Reserves

Ecosystem Management Exemplars

Galápagos Marine Reserve (MPA): Strictly regulates tourist entry numbers and charges mandatory entrance fees to directly fund ongoing marine conservation and enforcement against illegal fishing.

Sundarbans Mangrove Forest (India/Bangladesh): Stores ~4,000 metric tons of carbon per hectare. Threat from Hooghly River industrial runoff is managed via local seedling planting and patrols.
